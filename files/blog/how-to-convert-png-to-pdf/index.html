You've got a PNG — a screenshot, a scan, a receipt, a photo — and you need it as a PDF. Maybe a form only accepts PDFs. Maybe you want to email a batch of images as one tidy file. Or maybe you just want something that opens the same way on every device.

PNG is a great image format, but it's still just a picture. A PDF wraps that picture in a document anyone can open, print, or share without special software. And turning one into the other takes about ten seconds. Here's how.

Why turn a PNG into a PDF?

PNG files are sharp and easy to work with, but they're still just images. Open one on a different phone or computer and it might look fine — or it might not open at all if the app doesn't handle images well. A PDF doesn't have that problem. Every phone, computer, and browser opens PDFs, and they look the same everywhere.

The other big reason is packaging. A PDF can hold one image or fifty, all in one file, in the order you choose. That makes it the go-to format for sending scans, receipts, screenshots, and signed documents. One file, one attachment, done.

And PDFs print better. An image pasted into a document can stretch, crop, or move when it prints. A PDF page stays put — what you see on screen is what comes out of the printer.

  • A form, job application, or upload portal only accepts PDF files.
  • You want to send several images as one single file instead of a bunch of attachments.
  • You need something that prints cleanly, without the image shifting around.
  • You're sharing a scan or receipt and want it to look the same on every device.

PNG vs PDF: what's actually different?

Think of it this way: a PNG is a photo, and a PDF is an envelope you put the photo in. The photo doesn't change — the envelope just makes it easier to send, store, and print. Converting doesn't compress your image or throw away detail. The pixels stay exactly as they were.

PDFs also carry a bit of extra information — page size, margins, and the file structure — so the finished PDF is usually slightly bigger than the PNG you started with. That's normal, and it's a small price for a file that works everywhere.

One thing to know: a PNG turned into a PDF is still a picture inside a document. The text in a screenshot won't be selectable or searchable — it's an image, not typed text. If you need that, you'd need OCR software on top. For most jobs, though, the picture-as-PDF is exactly what's wanted.

  • PNG — a single image, great quality, but it's only a picture.
  • PDF — a document that can hold images, text, and many pages in one file.
  • A PNG opened as a PDF looks exactly the same — same pixels, same sharpness.
  • The one real trade-off: a PDF is a little bigger than the PNG inside it.

What to check after converting

Once you've got your PDF, give it a quick look before you send it off. Open it and flip through the pages. Check the images are in the order you wanted — this is the step most people skip, and it's the one that causes the most embarrassment when a receipt ends up before the cover page.

Check the quality too. The image should look just as sharp as the original PNG. If it looks blurry, the original PNG was probably small to begin with — converting can't add detail that was never there. Grab a higher-resolution version of the image if you can.

And check the file extension. It should end in .pdf. Some browsers save the converted file with the old name, and a form that sees ".png" in the name may still refuse it even though the file inside is a proper PDF.

  • Page order — if you added several images, check they're in the right order.
  • Quality — the image should look exactly the same as the original PNG.
  • Page size — each image gets its own page, sized to fit.
  • File name — check it ends in .pdf, not .png.

Common problems and quick fixes

The most common problem is a file that won't upload to the converter at all. Nine times out of ten, the file isn't really a PNG — sometimes an image gets renamed wrong when it moves between devices, or the download got cut off halfway. Try saving the image again from the original source, and check that it actually opens on your device first.

The second problem people run into is a PDF that looks too small on the page. That usually means the original PNG was small — a thumbnail, or a screenshot taken at low resolution. The PDF shows the image at its real size, so a small image sits in the middle of the page with white space around it. Resize the PNG to a bigger size first, or take the screenshot again at full resolution.

And the third one: your PDF is much bigger than you expected. That's normal when you convert several large PNGs at once — screenshots and photos add up fast. If the file is too big to email or upload, run it through a PDF compressor afterwards. It shrinks the file without hurting how the images look.

When a PDF is the wrong choice

A PDF is the right call most of the time, but not always. If you're posting the image on a website or social media, keep it as a PNG — browsers and apps handle images better than PDFs, and a PDF won't show up as a picture in a feed. Same goes for editing: if you still need to change the image itself, work on the PNG first and convert at the very end.

Also, if the file is just for you and you'll only ever open it on your own phone, a PNG is fine as it is. Converting to PDF only pays off when you need to share, print, or upload it somewhere that asks for a document. Otherwise you're just adding a step for no reason.

Frequently asked questions

Does converting PNG to PDF lose quality?

No. The converter keeps your image at full quality — the picture inside the PDF looks exactly like the original PNG. The PDF itself may be a little bigger than the PNG, but that's just the wrapper around it, not lost detail.

Can I convert multiple PNGs into one PDF?

Yes, you can convert several PNGs at once. Add all the images you want and the tool puts them into one PDF, one image per page, in the order you added them. Rearrange them before you convert if the order matters.

Is it safe to upload my images to the converter?

Yes. The converter runs entirely in your browser, so your images never leave your device. Nothing gets uploaded to a server or saved anywhere. Convert them, download the PDF, and you're done.

My PNG is small — will the PDF look tiny?

It will be — the PDF just shows the image at its real size. A small PNG gets a small picture on the page with white space around it. If you want it bigger, resize the PNG first, or use a PNG that was saved at a higher resolution.

Will the text in my image be selectable in the PDF?

A PNG is an image file, so it only holds a picture — no text you can select or copy. If you need selectable text, you need OCR software, which reads the text out of the picture. The PDF will look exactly like your image either way.

Can I convert PNG to PDF on my phone?

Yes. Open the tool on your phone's browser, upload the PNG from your photos or files, and download the PDF. No app to install, and it works the same on iPhone and Android.
