HEIC is the format iPhones use to save photos. It makes small, sharp files — but it only plays nicely inside Apple's world. PNG works everywhere. And turning one into the other takes about ten seconds, with no quality lost. Here's how.

What is HEIC, and why turn it into PNG?

HEIC is short for High Efficiency Image Container. Apple switched iPhones to it years ago because it stores photos in about half the space of JPG while keeping the same sharpness. Your phone can hold twice as many photos, and that's a real win.

The catch is that HEIC is still an Apple-first format. Windows, older Android phones, a lot of websites, and plenty of apps either can't open it at all or need extra software to do it. So the photo that looks perfect on your iPhone turns into an error message everywhere else.

PNG is the fix for that. It opens in every app, every browser, and every operating system — no extra software, no guessing. Converting a HEIC photo to PNG is basically repackaging the same pixels in a wrapper everything understands. The picture itself doesn't change at all.

  • You're moving iPhone photos to a Windows computer and they won't open.
  • A website or upload form rejects your photo because it's a .heic file.
  • You need to edit the photo in an app that doesn't support HEIC.
  • You're sending photos to someone who doesn't use an iPhone.

HEIC vs PNG: what's actually different?

The big difference is size and support. HEIC squeezes photos down much smaller, which is why Apple loves it — your phone storage goes a lot further. PNG files are bigger, sometimes two or three times the size of the same photo in HEIC. That's the price of working everywhere.

Quality-wise, there's nothing to worry about. Both formats store your photo without throwing away detail, so converting from HEIC to PNG keeps every pixel as sharp as it was. You won't see any difference between the two on screen.

PNG has one extra trick: it handles transparent backgrounds. iPhone photos don't have transparency, so it won't matter for most of your pictures — but for graphics or logos, PNG keeps those see-through areas intact.

  • HEIC — much smaller files, but mostly works inside Apple's world.
  • PNG — bigger files, opens everywhere, keeps transparency.
  • Both are lossless, so converting doesn't damage the photo.
  • The one real trade-off: your PNG will usually be bigger than the HEIC you started with.

What to check after converting

Once you've got your PNG, give it a quick look before you use it. Open it and check it looks the same as the original — same colors, same sharpness, same size on screen. If it looks right, you're done.

Don't be surprised if the PNG is a lot bigger than the HEIC. That's expected — PNG just doesn't compress as hard. A 2 MB HEIC photo can easily turn into a 5 or 6 MB PNG. If file size matters, you can always compress the PNG afterwards.

And this is the step most people skip: check the file extension. It should end in .png. Some browsers save the converted file with the old name, and an app that sees ".heic" in the name may still refuse it even though the file inside is a PNG.

  • File size — the PNG will usually be bigger than the HEIC. That's normal.
  • Quality — the photo should look exactly the same as the original.
  • Dimensions — the width and height should be exactly the same as the original.
  • File name — check it ends in .png, not .heic.

Common problems and quick fixes

The most common problem is a file that won't upload to the converter at all. Nine times out of ten, the file isn't really a HEIC — sometimes a photo gets renamed wrong when it moves between devices, or the transfer got cut off halfway. Try sending the photo to your computer again, and if it still won't go through, check that the file actually opens on your iPhone first.

The second problem people run into is a PNG that looks blurry. That usually means the original photo was small to begin with — maybe a thumbnail, or a photo that was already shrunk when it was sent to you. Converting can't add detail that was never there, so a small photo stays a small photo. Grab the full-size original from your iPhone if you can.

And the third one: your app still won't open the file after converting. Check the extension first — it should end in .png. If it does, try opening the PNG in your browser. If the browser shows it fine, the file is good and the app is the problem, not your photo.

Frequently asked questions

Does converting HEIC to PNG lose quality?

No. HEIC and PNG are both lossless formats, so the pixels stay exactly the same when you convert. The PNG file will usually be bigger than the HEIC you started with, but that's just the file size — the picture itself doesn't lose any detail.

Can I convert HEIC to PNG on my iPhone?

Yes. The converter runs in your browser, so it works on iPhone, Android, and any phone browser. Open the tool on your iPhone, upload the .heic photo, and download the PNG — no app to install.

Why is the PNG file bigger than the HEIC?

HEIC was built to squeeze photos into the smallest possible file, while PNG was built to keep every pixel perfect and work everywhere. A bigger file is the normal trade-off for that wider support — a 2 MB HEIC photo can easily become a 5 or 6 MB PNG.

Will my PNG keep its transparent background?

iPhone photos don't have transparent backgrounds, so there's nothing to lose there. But PNG does support transparency, so if your HEIC file ever does have see-through areas, the PNG will keep them.

Is it safe to upload my photos to the converter?

Yes. The converter runs entirely in your browser, so your photo never leaves your device. Nothing gets uploaded to a server or saved anywhere. Convert it, download the PNG, and you're done.

Can I convert HEIC to JPG instead?

Yes — and it's the same quick process. If you want a smaller file for sharing or uploading, run your HEIC photo through the free HEIC to JPG converter instead. You'll get a much smaller file that still looks great.
