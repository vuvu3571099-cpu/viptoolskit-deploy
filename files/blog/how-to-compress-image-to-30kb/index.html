How to Compress an Image to 30KB (Without Making It Unreadable)

Quick answer

Resize the image to the small dimensions the form asks for (often around 200 pixels wide for a photo, 140 for a signature), then lower the JPG quality to about 50–65 until the file drops under 30KB. Resizing does most of the work — quality alone won't get you there.

You filled out the whole form, uploaded everything, and then it happened: "file size must be less than 30KB." And the photo on your phone is 3MB — a hundred times too big. Exam portals, job applications, and government sites love this limit, and none of them explain how you're supposed to hit it.

Here's the honest truth first: 30KB is a tight limit, and you won't get there by nudging a quality slider. You need to shrink the image's dimensions too. But once you know the two levers — size, then quality — it takes about a minute.

Why so many forms ask for 30KB

If you've ever applied for a government job, registered for an exam, or filled out a university admission form, you've met this number. 30KB is the classic limit for passport photos and scanned signatures — the form stores millions of these little images, so it wants each one tiny.

Phone cameras don't help. A single photo runs 2MB to 6MB — 60 to 200 times the limit — so the file you need is almost always too big straight out of the camera.

What actually makes an image file smaller

Three things decide how big an image file is: the format, the dimensions, and the quality setting. At 30KB you need all three working together, and dimensions matter most.

Dimensions come first because they decide how many pixels the file has to store. A photo that's 4000 pixels wide holds sixteen times the pixels of one that's 1000 wide. But the form shows your photo at maybe 200 pixels wide, so those extra pixels are just dead weight. Cutting the width down to what the form actually asks for can drop a 3MB photo to under 100KB before you even touch the quality slider.

Format comes next. A JPG of a photo is far smaller than a PNG of the same photo, because JPG drops tiny details your eyes can't see anyway. PNG keeps every pixel perfect, which is great for screenshots and logos — and terrible for file size. If your file is a PNG, convert it to JPG first and watch the size fall.

Then there's quality. At quality 90 a JPG keeps almost everything; at 60 it drops details you can't see at small sizes, and the file can shrink by half again. At 30KB you'll work lower than most guides suggest, and that's fine — at the tiny size these photos are shown, quality 50 to 65 still looks clean.

  • Dimensions: the width and height in pixels — this is the big one at 30KB.
  • Format: JPG for photos and signatures; PNG makes much bigger files.
  • Quality: 50–65 is the range that usually lands under 30KB.

Getting under 30KB without wrecking the photo

The biggest mistake is crushing the quality slider while the photo is still full-size. A 4000-pixel photo at quality 30 still won't be 30KB — and it'll look awful. Resize first: once the photo is 200 pixels wide, quality 60 will usually land you under 30KB.

Work down in small steps — 65, then 60, then 55 — and stop the moment the file dips under 30KB. And check the result at the size the form will show it: a photo that looks a bit soft at full screen can look fine at 200 pixels wide.

And keep a copy of the original. Once you compress a photo, the detail it lost is gone for good, so save the small version under a different name.

  • Resize first, then touch quality — doing it the other way round wastes your time.
  • Lower the quality in small steps and stop as soon as you hit 30KB.
  • Always keep the original file, and save the compressed copy separately.

Why your image is still over 30KB

Sometimes you follow the steps and the file just won't budge. Here's what's usually going on.

First, check the format. If your file is a PNG, it'll fight you — PNGs barely shrink no matter how far you drag the slider, because the format doesn't discard detail the way JPG does. If the image is a photo or a scanned signature, convert it to JPG and watch the size fall. If it has to stay PNG, shrinking the dimensions is your only real lever.

Second, look at the dimensions. If you skipped the resize step, no quality setting will save you — a photo that's still 2000 pixels wide holds far too many pixels for a 30KB file. Bring the width down to 200 or so and try again.

Third, some photos are just busy — a crowd scene, a forest, a photo full of fine detail. Those compress worse than a simple headshot, so you'll need a smaller width and a lower quality setting. And if it's a signature, scan it in black and white — it compresses far smaller than colour.

When 30KB isn't the right target

Don't shrink every photo down to 30KB just because you can — match the limit you're actually given, not the smallest number you've heard.

And never use a 30KB file for anything that gets printed. It looks fine in a small box on a screen, but soft and blurry on paper, where every pixel counts. Keep the full-size original for print.

But for online forms and job portals that cap uploads at 30KB, this is all you need: resize, save as JPG, and ease the quality down until the file slips under the limit.

Frequently asked questions

Will my image still look okay at 30KB?

For a small photo or a signature, yes — if you resize it down first. At the size forms actually show it, a 30KB photo looks perfectly fine. Just don't expect it to survive being printed large or zoomed in.

Can any image be compressed to 30KB?

Mostly, yes — but not every photo. A plain headshot against a light background, a scanned signature, or a simple document scan can reach 30KB easily. A big, detailed photo full of trees, crowds, or patterns is much harder to squeeze that far, and you may have to shrink it very small to get there.

What's the fastest way to get an image under 30KB?

Start with the dimensions. Most 30KB limits come with a size rule too — something like 200 x 230 pixels for a photo or 140 x 60 for a signature. Resize the image to those exact dimensions first, then lower the quality until the file drops under 30KB. Resizing does most of the work; quality does the rest.

Why is my image still over 30KB after compressing?

Check the format first. If your file is a PNG, convert it to JPG — a PNG of the same photo can be three or four times bigger, and it barely shrinks no matter how far you drag the quality slider. Also check the dimensions: a 4000-pixel photo won't hit 30KB on quality alone. Bring the width down and try again.

Is it safe to compress a passport photo or signature online?

Yes — the compressor in this guide runs entirely in your browser. Your photo never leaves your computer, so nothing gets uploaded to a server. That's worth checking with any online tool, especially for ID photos: if it asks you to wait for an upload, your file is going to their server.

How small should I make a passport photo or signature?

A passport photo for an online form is usually fine at 100–200 pixels wide, because the form only shows it small. A signature can be even smaller — around 140 pixels wide is the standard on most government and exam portals.
