You plug in your iPhone, copy your photos to your Windows 11 PC, and then — nothing. Double-click a photo and Windows says it can't open the file, or it asks you to pay for an extension. The photo is right there, and you still can't see it.

The file is a HEIC photo, Apple's default format on iPhones since 2017. Windows 11 doesn't come with the codec to read it. But you don't need to buy anything or install sketchy software to fix this — here are four ways that actually work, starting with the fastest.

Why Windows 11 won't open your HEIC file

A HEIC file is a photo saved in the HEIF format, which Apple made the default on iPhones back in 2017. It keeps the same quality as a JPG in about half the file size, which is great for your phone's storage. The catch is that the format is built on HEVC video compression, and Microsoft doesn't bundle the HEVC codec with Windows.

So when you double-click a HEIC file, the Photos app doesn't know what to do with it. It either shows an error or pops up a message asking you to install the HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store. That message is the whole problem in one sentence — Windows can open the file, it just needs a small add-on first.

And no, your photo isn't broken. The file is fine. It's your PC that's missing one piece of software, and there are a few ways to get past it — free or nearly free.

Method 1: Install the official HEIF and HEVC extensions

This is the proper fix if you get HEIC files from an iPhone all the time. Once the extensions are in, HEIC photos open in the Photos app like any other picture, and you even get thumbnails in File Explorer. You need two of them, and this is the step most people skip — installing only the first one and wondering why nothing changed.

  1. Open the Microsoft Store on your PC and search for "HEIF Image Extensions".
  2. Install it (about $0.99). This one teaches Windows the HEIF photo format.
  3. Now search for "HEVC Video Extensions" and install that too (about $0.99). This is the codec that actually decodes the photo.
  4. Close the Photos app completely and open it again.
  5. Double-click your HEIC file — it should open normally now.
  • Total cost: about $2 for both extensions, one-time payment.
  • Bonus: Microsoft also lists a free version called "HEVC Video Extensions from Device Manufacturer" — it's the same codec, but it's hard to find in Store search. If you spot it, grab it and skip the paid one.
  • If the Store asks you to sign in, use your normal Microsoft account — the same one your PC already uses.

Method 3: Free viewers that open HEIC without paying

One warning: only download these from their official websites. A random "free HEIC viewer" from a search ad can come bundled with junk software you didn't ask for. If a viewer asks for your credit card for a "free" download, close the tab.

  • CopyTrans HEIC for Windows — free, and it adds HEIC support straight into the Photos app plus thumbnails in File Explorer. This is the one most people end up using.
  • IrfanView with its plugins — a tiny, fast image viewer that's been around for years. Install the app, then the plugin pack, and HEIC files open fine.
  • Google Photos or Dropbox in your browser — upload the HEIC file and it previews right there on the web page. Handy if the photo is already in your cloud storage.
  • VLC media player — newer versions can open HEIC files too, though it's a video player first, so the photo tools are basic.

Method 4: Stop your iPhone making HEIC files

The best fix for the future is to stop the problem at the source. Your iPhone can save photos as plain JPG instead of HEIC, and then this whole issue never comes up again. It takes ten seconds to change.

  1. On your iPhone, open Settings, then Camera, then Formats.
  2. Tap Most Compatible instead of High Efficiency. New photos will save as JPG.
  3. Also go to Settings > Photos, scroll down to Transfer to Mac or PC, and pick Automatic — that way photos sent to your computer by cable arrive as JPG too.
  • This only changes new photos. The HEIC files already on your phone stay HEIC, so use Method 2 or 3 for those.
  • JPG photos take up more space on your phone, roughly twice as much. If storage is tight, keep HEIC on the phone and convert the ones you send to your PC instead.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Windows 11 ask me to pay to open HEIC files?

It's about the codec, not the file. HEIC photos use a compression format (HEVC) that Microsoft doesn't include with Windows for free, because of licensing costs. So the Photos app asks you to buy the HEIF Image Extensions and HEVC Video Extensions from the Microsoft Store — about $0.99 each. It's annoying, but that's the official reason.

Can I open HEIC files on Windows 11 for free?

Yes. The quickest free way is to convert the HEIC file to JPG in your browser — no install, no payment, and the photo opens everywhere after that. If you open HEIC files a lot, a free viewer like CopyTrans HEIC adds support to the Photos app and File Explorer without costing anything.

Will converting HEIC to JPG lose quality?

A little, but you won't notice it. JPG uses slightly stronger compression than HEIC, so a converted photo is technically a tiny bit less detailed. In normal viewing — on screen, in a message, printed at normal sizes — the difference is invisible. Keep the original HEIC file if you plan to edit the photo heavily later.

How do I open HEIC files in the Windows 11 Photos app?

Install the two Microsoft Store extensions — HEIF Image Extensions and HEVC Video Extensions — then close and reopen the Photos app. After that, double-clicking a HEIC file opens it like any other photo. If it still won't open, check the file actually ends in .heic and isn't a half-finished transfer from your phone.

Can I change my iPhone so photos save as JPG?

Yes. On your iPhone go to Settings > Camera > Formats and pick Most Compatible. New photos will save as JPG instead of HEIC. Photos you already took stay HEIC, so you'll still need one of the methods above for those. And if you transfer photos by cable, set Settings > Photos > Transfer to Mac or PC to Automatic so they arrive as JPG.

Is a HEIC file a virus or unsafe?

No. HEIC is just a photo format, the same as JPG or PNG. The file itself is safe. What you do need to watch out for is random free converter apps and websites that bundle extra software or ask for payment after one free file. Stick to a converter that runs in your browser and doesn't ask you to install anything.
