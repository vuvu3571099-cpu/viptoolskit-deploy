AVIF vs WebP: Which Image Format Is Better?

Quick answer

AVIF makes smaller files at the same quality — often 20–30% smaller than WebP. But WebP works in almost every browser and app, while AVIF still breaks on older devices. For a website, use AVIF with a WebP fallback; for sharing files, WebP is the safer pick.

You've probably seen both of these formats popping up everywhere — AVIF and WebP. They promise smaller files and sharper pictures than good old JPEG, and if you're wondering which one to pick, the choice can feel confusing.

There isn't one winner for everything. AVIF squeezes more quality into fewer bytes; WebP works in more places. This guide compares them head to head, so you can pick the right one for what you're doing.

The short version: AVIF wins on quality, WebP wins on reach

If you only remember one thing, make it this: AVIF makes smaller files at the same quality, but WebP works almost everywhere. AVIF is the newer, better compressor; WebP is the safer choice.

For a website, the smart move is usually AVIF first, with WebP as the fallback. Browsers that understand AVIF get the tiny file; everyone else gets WebP and the page still looks right. And for sharing images with other people — email, chat, social media — WebP or even JPEG is still the safer pick, because plenty of apps still can't open AVIF.

What AVIF and WebP actually are

Both formats were built to fix the same problem: JPEG is old. It dates from the early 1990s and wastes a lot of space. WebP arrived from Google in 2010, and AVIF came later, built on the AV1 video codec behind Netflix and YouTube.

That family tree matters. AVIF borrows tricks from modern video compression, which is why it packs a photo into such a small file. WebP is simpler and older — but that age is also its strength, because nearly every browser and app has had years to add support.

Both handle the stuff JPEG can't: transparency for see-through backgrounds, and lossless compression that saves a file without losing any detail. WebP also does animation, which is why it often replaces GIFs. AVIF can animate too, but almost nobody uses it that way yet.

AVIF vs WebP: file size and image quality

This is where AVIF pulls ahead. Save the same photo in both formats at the same quality and the AVIF file will usually be a fifth to a third smaller. The gap gets bigger on detailed photos — outdoor shots, portraits with lots of texture — because that's where AVIF's newer compression tricks shine.

Look closely and you'll see it. AVIF holds on to fine detail better: strands of hair, leaves on a tree, the weave of a fabric. WebP is good — miles ahead of JPEG — but at very small file sizes it starts to smear those fine details into a soft blur, while AVIF keeps them readable.

AVIF also supports HDR photos and 10-bit and 12-bit colour, which means smoother gradients — no banding in skies. WebP is stuck at 8-bit colour, same as JPEG. For most photos that won't matter, but for a photographer's portfolio or a homepage hero image, it can.

  • Same photo, same quality: AVIF files are usually 20–30% smaller than WebP.
  • Compared to JPEG, WebP is around 25–35% smaller; AVIF is often 50% smaller.
  • AVIF keeps fine detail better — hair, leaves, fabric — where WebP can start to smudge.
  • AVIF supports HDR and wider colour ranges; WebP tops out at 8-bit colour.

Browser support: where each format actually works

Here's where WebP fights back. It works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, and Opera — basically every browser made in the last few years. Put a WebP image on your site today and almost everyone will see it.

AVIF support is good, but it's newer. Chrome and Firefox have had it since 2020 and 2021, and Safari joined in late 2022. That covers most people now — but anyone on an older iPhone or an old Safari will see a broken image instead of your photo. And that's the kind of bug you only find out about when a visitor complains.

It's not just browsers either — plenty of apps still can't open AVIF: some image editors, some chat apps, some older phones. WebP has had a decade to spread into all those places. So if the file needs to travel — email, group chat, social media — WebP is the one that will actually open.

  • WebP: Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, Opera — supported nearly everywhere since 2020.
  • AVIF: Chrome 85+, Firefox 93+, Safari 16+, Edge — good, but older devices miss out.

Which one should you use?

It depends on what the image is for. There's no single right answer, so here's how to decide in about ten seconds.

For a website, the picture element makes the fallback easy: the browser picks AVIF if it can, WebP if it can't, and JPEG if it understands neither. Most site builders handle this for you now.

The one mistake to avoid is serving AVIF with no fallback. A broken image icon is worse than a slightly bigger file — always give the browser a second option.

  • Website photos: AVIF first, WebP as fallback — you get the smallest files without breaking anything.
  • Logos and graphics with transparency: either works; WebP is the safer pick.
  • Animations: WebP — it's the established replacement for GIFs.
  • Sharing or emailing a photo: WebP or JPEG, so the other person can actually open it.
  • Storing photos for yourself: AVIF, if your apps support it — it keeps the most quality per megabyte.

Frequently asked questions

Is AVIF better than WebP?

For quality and file size, yes. AVIF makes smaller files at the same quality and keeps fine detail better. But WebP works in more browsers and apps, so it's still the safer choice when compatibility matters. For most websites, AVIF with a WebP fallback is the best setup.

Is AVIF supported in all browsers?

Not quite. Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari 16 and newer support AVIF. Older Safari and older iPhones don't, so those visitors see a broken image without a fallback. WebP works in pretty much every browser made since 2020.

Should I use AVIF or WebP for my website?

Use both. Serve AVIF first with WebP as the fallback, and your pages load fast for modern browsers without breaking for older ones. If your site builder only lets you pick one format, pick WebP.

Does AVIF support transparency?

Yes — AVIF supports transparency just like WebP and PNG, so it works for logos and graphics with see-through backgrounds. It also supports lossless compression, so nothing is lost in the save.

Why won't my AVIF file open?

The app or device you're using probably doesn't support AVIF yet. Older phones, some image editors, and some chat apps can't open it. Convert the file to WebP or JPG and it'll open anywhere — the converter in this guide does it in seconds.

Which format is smaller, AVIF or WebP?

AVIF, usually by a clear margin. At the same quality, an AVIF file is often 20–30% smaller than the same image saved as WebP, and around half the size of a JPEG. The exact saving depends on the photo — detailed images save more.
